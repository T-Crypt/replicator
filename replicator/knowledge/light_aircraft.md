Orientation: the aircraft lies along Z with the nose at +Z, wings along X, up is Y; it rests on its landing gear
at y = 0. Use the `wing` op for the main wing, horizontal tail and fin (a fin is a wing with "mirror": false
rotated 90 degrees about Z), `loft` or `revolve` for the fuselage and spinner, `rotor` for the propeller.

Reference: a Cessna 172 is about 11.0 m span, 8.3 m long and 2.7 m high, with a wing chord about 1.5 m and a
propeller about 1.9 m across. Use these proportions for a four-seat light aircraft.

Proportions and layout (conservative):
- Wing aspect ratio (span / mean chord) about 6 to 9 for light aircraft, 15 to 30 for gliders. Dihedral 1 to 5
  degrees; sweep near 0 for propeller aircraft. Wing thickness about 12 to 15 % of chord.
- High wing (on top of the cabin) or low wing (under the floor). The wing sits roughly a quarter to a third of the
  way back from the nose; the centre of gravity is near the wing's quarter chord.
- Horizontal tail span about a third of the wing span, its area about 15 to 25 % of the wing area; it sits at the
  very back, about 2.5 to 3 mean chords behind the wing. Vertical fin about half the tail span tall.
- Fuselage widest at the cabin (about 1.0 to 1.2 m for side-by-side seats) and tapering to the tail.
- Tricycle gear: a nose wheel and two main wheels just behind the centre of gravity; wheels about 400 to 500 across.

Materials: painted aluminium skin, clear canopy (glass), black rubber tyres, a metal or composite propeller.
