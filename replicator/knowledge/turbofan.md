---
title: Turbofan and jet engine
keywords: turbofan, jet engine, jet, turbojet, turboprop, gas turbine, aircraft engine, fan engine, afterburner, thrust reverser, nacelle
---
Orientation: lay the engine along Z, intake at the front (+Z). Rotating stages are `rotor` ops (built in the XZ plane around Y) given "rotate": [90, 0, 0] so they spin around Z. Casings are `tube` ops rotated the same way.

Parts, front to back:
- Spinner: a cone or ogive on the fan hub.
- Fan: wide-chord, strongly twisted blades (modern engines about 16 to 24 blades; older ones 30 to 40), tip close to the casing.
- Nacelle: inlet lip (thick rounded leading edge), fan cowl, then the thrust reverser section, then the bypass (fan) nozzle. The nacelle is hollow; its inner wall is the bypass duct's outer wall.
- Outlet guide vanes: a ring of stator vanes behind the fan, usually more vanes than fan blades, bridging the core casing to the nacelle.
- Core, inside the bypass duct: low-pressure compressor (booster), high-pressure compressor (many stages, blades get shorter toward the back), annular combustor, high-pressure turbine (1 or 2 stages), low-pressure turbine (several stages, getting larger toward the back).
- Core nozzle and tail cone (plug) at the very back.
- Accessory gearbox on the underside of the fan case or core, driven by a radial shaft.
- Pylon on top, carrying the engine.

Proportions:
- Bypass ratio 5 to 12 on modern airliner engines: the core is small and the fan is most of the frontal area.
- Overall length is about 1.5 to 2 times the fan diameter. The core diameter is roughly a third to a half of the fan diameter.
- Reference: the GE90-115B fan is about 3.25 m across; the CFM56 fan is about 1.5 to 1.75 m.
- Thrust reverser: cascades under translating cowl sleeves, or pivoting doors in the bypass duct.

Materials: titanium fan blades (or carbon composite with a titanium edge), aluminium or composite nacelle, nickel alloy hot section, heat-tinted dark core nozzle.
