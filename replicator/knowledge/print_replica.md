---
title: Printing a replica on a Bambu bed
keywords: 3d print, 3d printer, 3d printed, printable, print replica, replica, printer, fdm, filament, bambu
---
Replicator prints at whatever scale fits the bed (its fit functions scale the build uniformly), so design the part at full size and the app fits it. These facts keep a design printable at any scale.

Beds: the Bambu X1 Carbon, P1S, P1P and A1 build 256 x 256 x 256 mm; the A1 mini 180 x 180 x 180; the H2D about 350 x 320 x 325 (published figures, to verify on the device). A 2 mm safety margin is kept off the walls and the gantry.

Walls and detail: a 0.4 mm nozzle (0.2 on the small machines) is the smallest feature that prints at all. Give every wall at least 0.8 mm (two nozzle lines) and load-bearing parts 1.2 to 2 mm, at full scale. Anything thinner than one nozzle line does not print: railings, mullions, pins and thin fins vanish, and text smaller than about 1 mm disappears, so give details a printable minimum or drop them.

Orientation: the part lies flat on the bed. Keep the build's base (its Y-up ground face) flat and as wide as you can, and keep overhangs within about 45 degrees of horizontal or they need supports; prefer drafted, braced shapes. Horizontal spans (bridges) print without support.

Scaling: a 1:50 replica of a house is a few tens of millimetres across, so windows, doors and anything smaller than roughly 5 mm at full scale become unprintable slivers; a print-ready design keeps its details chunky. The build stands on its own flat base; a tall thin part (a model rocket) prints standing up, never lying on its side.
